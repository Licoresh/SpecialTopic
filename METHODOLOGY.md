# Methodology: Philippine employer skills and PSF-AAI

## Research question and scope

To what extent do skills and competencies required in Philippine AI- and analytics-related job advertisements align with the Philippine Skills Framework for Analytics and Artificial Intelligence (PSF-AAI)? The analysis covers Philippine job ads and a researcher-verified PSF-AAI reference.

## System workflow at a glance

The application supports a researcher-led, descriptive comparison of job-advertised skills with the verified PSF-AAI reference. It prepares and organizes evidence; it does not determine whether the sample is representative or certify that an AI suggestion is correct.

| Stage | System role | Researcher decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare data | Map source columns, clean job records, preserve identifiers and source evidence, and flag possible duplicates. | Confirm Philippine provenance, AI/analytics relevance, inclusion, and duplicate handling. |
| Verify framework | Extract a reviewable PSF-AAI reference from the official source and retain page and source provenance. | Check competency names, descriptions, codes, roles, levels, and cited pages before activation. |
| Discover phrases | Find candidate skill phrases and retain normalized text, job-ad occurrence, and context. | Inspect context, refine phrases, and exclude irrelevant candidates. |
| Label and map | Keep analytical category labels separate from PSF-AAI links; produce model-assisted drafts and candidate matches. | Review each category and each phrase-ad mapping. Multiple competencies require separate source-based rationales. |
| Analyze and report | Rebuild results from current study inputs and reviewed occurrence decisions; calculate demand and alignment summaries. | Check denominators, source coverage, stale evidence, and limitations before interpreting or reporting results. |

AI-generated candidates remain suggestions until a researcher saves a decision. A category applies to a normalized phrase across its advertisements; a PSF-AAI decision applies only to the reviewed phrase in its specific advertisement.

## Data and units

The primary observational unit is a unique job advertisement identified by its source record ID. Researcher screening must establish Philippine provenance and AI/analytics relevance; a Philippine location alone is insufficient. The app records an explicit scope review before extraction from uploaded job data. Keep source URL, collection details and raw file hash. Confirm duplicates before cleaning, and report source coverage and sampling dates. The skill-demand unit is a distinct job ad mentioning a normalized skill phrase; repeated mentions in one ad count once.

The PSF-AAI reference stores competency name and description, functional/enabling category, official competency codes, framework career-role links and listed levels, source PDF page and URL, source hash, reviewer, review status and timestamp. When an explicitly labeled Range of Application is present in a verified reference table, it is retained as additional context for semantic candidate ranking and researcher review; it does not create another competency or independently establish a match. The bundled TESDA PDF edition has no separate Range of Application field, so that field remains blank unless supplied and verified from an authoritative source. PDF extraction produces a reviewable draft. It is not an authoritative active taxonomy until a researcher checks definitions, codes, roles and levels against their cited pages. Aliases and keywords are not inferred from prose.

This study does not collect or compare university curricula. Its comparison is employer-stated job-ad skills against the researcher-verified TESDA PSF-AAI reference.

## Job text preparation and phrase discovery

Map source columns to the app's job schema; preserve raw bytes and source fields. Cleaning normalizes HTML, Unicode and whitespace while retaining technical terms such as C++, C#, .NET, R, SQL and Power BI. Empty fields stay empty. A reviewer confirms duplicate removals.

The existing NLP pipeline combines exact framework terms and researcher-supplied aliases/keywords with general discovery vocabulary and spaCy lookup lemmatization over candidate n-grams. Every candidate retains its original phrase, normalized phrase, job ID and source context. Candidate limits are recorded. General vocabulary and model predictions are discovery evidence, not human labels or PSF matches. Researchers inspect context, edit phrases and may label a candidate IRRELEVANT.

## Saved results and reproducibility

A published extraction retains candidate and match records, discovery diagnostics, a creation timestamp and an input fingerprint. The fingerprint covers the cleaned job advertisements and PSF-AAI reference, active upload hashes, column mappings, saved category annotations, settings and selected-model metadata. New extraction runs also record the producing software version and an extraction-pipeline revision. The pipeline revision identifies changes in extraction, matching or classification behavior; interface changes have a separate software version.

Loading saved results retrieves existing extraction records without repeating text processing, matching, classifier inference or training. Results may be reused after compatible interface updates when their research-input fingerprint still matches. Changes to research inputs or the extraction pipeline require another extraction before the Results pages and research exports use them. The Results and Research Readiness pages apply the same freshness check. For supported older runs whose fingerprint included the application version, the system reconstructs fingerprints for explicitly compatible releases using the current research inputs and requires an exact match. Loading does not replace the original extraction timestamp or rewrite its historical metadata.

Result reuse is an operational measure for repeatable analysis and reduced processing time. It does not independently validate the source advertisements, researcher labels, classifier performance or competency mappings. Saved occurrence-level PSF-AAI reviews are incorporated when analysis tables are rebuilt; each review must still match its source evidence and reference. Updating these reviews is distinct from rerunning the extraction pipeline.

## Manual labels and supervised classification

### Analytical category hierarchy

Each normalized phrase receives one researcher-reviewed leaf category. These are study-defined analytical categories, distinct from the official PSF-AAI reference categories. Technical / Digital is the parent of AI / Machine Learning, Data / Analytics, and general programming and digital tools. Enabling capabilities contains communication and personal effectiveness (stored as Soft / Enabling) and Leadership / Management. Business / Domain represents domain knowledge. Education / Qualification is a credential requirement, not a competency. IRRELEVANT identifies excluded material. The legacy stored value Technical / Digital denotes the general programming and digital-tools leaf; the interface displays its full hierarchy.

Researchers select the most specific applicable leaf using source context and the decision rules in CATEGORY_CODEBOOK.md. Parent categories and requirement types are derived from the leaf. Parent and child totals must not be added together; job-level parent counts require deduplication within the parent. Credentials should be reported separately from competency frequencies. Existing labels are not automatically re-reviewed by changing the codebook.

### AI-assisted suggestions and researcher review

The pinned BAAI/bge-m3 embedding model (revision 5617a9f61b028005a4858fdac845db406aefb181) ranks the study's category descriptions and competencies in the active PSF-AAI taxonomy using a phrase and nearby source context. Exact project-vocabulary rules take priority for known phrases. Blank and symbol-only phrases are excluded from review display and cannot receive competency suggestions. BGE-M3 similarities are rankings, not calibrated probabilities or accuracy measures.

For each eligible phrase occurrence, the application can present one category draft and up to three ranked PSF-AAI candidates. The candidates are prompts for review, not automatic labels or approved mappings. Researchers inspect the job-ad evidence, choose the category and any applicable competencies, provide a separate rationale for each competency, and save their decisions. Similarity scores only rank candidates; they are not probabilities, accuracy estimates, or evidence that every suggested competency applies.

Only BGE-M3 suggestions and researcher review informed the study's model-assisted labeling process. No separate chat-model context check was used to generate, check, or determine study labels, mappings, or results.

Candidate embeddings are reused for category and PSF ranking, and completed draft batches are retained. These are operational measures, not evidence of improved research accuracy. BGE-M3 is not fine-tuned when researchers save annotations. The suggestion workflow requires evaluation against independently reviewed examples before claims of improved classification quality.

### Researcher review and training observations

For a new phrase, the category field begins blank; unreviewed phrase-ad occurrences have no researcher-approved PSF-AAI links. AI drafts are read-only guidance beside the researcher-controlled fields in the same review table. Researchers choose values and explicitly save; drafts are never copied into labels automatically. A category decision applies to a normalized phrase across its advertisements, while PSF-AAI links are decided for each phrase-ad occurrence and may include multiple competencies. Each selected competency requires its own source-based rationale and confirmation. Saved decisions record reviewer identity and timestamp, but the app cannot establish that a reviewer actually checked the evidence or that a decision is correct. PSF assignment is optional and remains distinct from the analytical category.

Only reviewed Philippine job-ad phrases passing provenance and conflict validation qualify for research training. A normalized phrase has one category across occurrences; researchers must resolve disagreements and refine ambiguous phrases using source context. AI drafts alone and synthetic software fixtures do not qualify as research training observations.

The explicit training action compares TF-IDF pipelines with Multinomial Naive Bayes and balanced Logistic Regression. Training prefers `normalized_phrase`, falling back to `phrase`. When source IDs are present, the holdout and cross-validation split by job-ad source ID so wording from one ad cannot appear in both training and evaluation. Training is blocked if a source-disjoint holdout cannot retain every category in both partitions. If source IDs are unavailable in a non-production software fixture, the fallback phrase-level split is explicitly identified and must not be treated as research evaluation. Model selection prioritizes macro F1, then weighted F1, per-class performance and CV stability. Artifacts include fitted pipelines and data/configuration hashes. A grouped holdout is only an internal estimate for the sampled postings; it does not validate extraction, PSF mapping, label quality, representativeness, or performance on a separately collected sample.

Operational gates require at least 30 distinct usable phrases, two classes, five phrases per class, no conflicting phrase labels, complete provenance and a feasible source-disjoint split. These are safeguards, not a claim of statistical adequacy. The Research Readiness page additionally flags source concentration, reviewer-ID variants, short phrase fragments and phrase-level evaluation leakage. It cannot establish annotation quality or a representative sampling frame. Researchers should seek independent labels, adjudicate disagreement, document inter-rater agreement, and evaluate on a genuinely independent sample. A separate `models/provisional/` artifact is reserved for weak-supervision experiments and is never used by the production classifier or reported as a research model.

## Employer-to-PSF mapping and alignment

Mapping uses a many-to-many relationship between extracted skill occurrences and competencies in the active PSF-AAI reference. An occurrence is one distinct normalized skill phrase within one job advertisement; repeated mentions of that phrase within the same advertisement count once. Several distinct phrases may map to the same competency, including phrases from different advertisements. The same phrase may have different mappings in different advertisements. Each link retains the extraction ID, source advertisement ID, original text, available character offsets, advertisement title and URL, and the target competency ID and reference evidence. Advertisement URLs and framework-reference URLs are stored separately.

The active official-PDF importer retains each competency's short definition plus the continuation-page proficiency descriptions, underpinning knowledge and skills-application text. It keeps Range of Application separately and records the competency heading page, source URL and PDF hash. Exact competency names, researcher-provided aliases/keywords, optional local semantic evidence or fuzzy similarity supply at most one automatic candidate per occurrence. Phrase matches found in the competency profile prose (including proficiency and application text) or Range of Application are marked PARTIALLY ALIGNED as supporting evidence, not as direct title matches; profile-token matching is heuristic and requires researcher review. Separately, the model-assisted manual-review interface presents up to three distinct PSF-AAI candidates ranked by BGE-M3 similarity between the phrase with nearby context and the framework competency name, description, and any verified Range of Application text. Three is an interface shortlist limit, not a validated classification threshold. Candidate names, IDs and ranking scores are persisted as AI drafts; existing single-candidate drafts remain readable. The shortlist neither assigns multiple mappings nor establishes that all listed competencies apply. Researchers independently select and justify every saved link. Additional competencies require researcher review of that occurrence. Researchers select each applicable competency, supply a separate competency-specific rationale, and confirm that the source context supports every link. Broad or ambiguous wording alone is insufficient grounds for multiple assignments. An explicit no-match decision is also supported. The interface checks for missing and duplicated rationales; it cannot establish their substantive validity, which remains the researcher's responsibility. Saving a decision changes only that occurrence. Manual Labeling presents category and multiple PSF-AAI selections together in a review table. By default, one row represents a distinct normalized phrase and displays a representative advertisement plus the number of source advertisements containing it. An alternative occurrence view allows inspection of the phrase in each advertisement. Grouping the review display does not remove source occurrences or change demand frequencies. Categories are assigned per normalized phrase, while multiple PSF-AAI links are reviewed per phrase and advertisement using the same occurrence-review store as PSF Mapping. Category-only saves preserve each source occurrence’s existing legacy PSF reference without copying it to other advertisements. Occurrence reviews take precedence in the research analysis.

Reviewed links record reviewer identity, timestamp, decision version, and hashes of the source context and active reference. Persisted PSF-AAI decisions remain visible with the selected competencies, reviewer and save time; an existing decision can be updated. An explicit reviewed no-match remains distinguishable from an unreviewed occurrence. Shared saves are reported as confirmed only when the database returns the expected new decision version; an uncertain response requires checking the persisted decision before retrying. A changed source or reference invalidates the prior decision until re-reviewed. Shared reviews use version checks to reject concurrent overwrites and retain decision history. Mapping results and exports include the PSF competency profile and, when available in the verified source, Range of Application as the visible reference basis for each comparison. Results also retain career role, reference category and listed level, source page/URL/hash, match method and similarity fields. Similarity is a heuristic, not a calibrated correctness probability. A researcher-selected exact reference is recorded as a human assignment; its exact-match score is not an independent validation metric. A listed reference level does not imply applicant proficiency. Education and qualification requirements are analyzed separately from competency demand and are excluded from the PSF skills-gap calculation.

Competency demand counts distinct advertisements, distinct skill phrases, and distinct occurrences separately. Several links from one advertisement to the same competency count as one advertisement. Competency counts overlap and must not be summed as a unique-advertisement total. Phrase-level alignment rates count each normalized phrase once, using its strongest observed status across links and advertisements (aligned, then partially aligned, then not mapped, then unclassified). This summary describes whether a phrase has supporting alignment anywhere in the selected sample; it does not imply that all its occurrences align. The occurrence mapping table preserves mixed and stale decisions for inspection. Multiple links do not increase the phrase-rate denominator.

Default thresholds are configurable: score ≥0.80 ALIGNED; ≥0.60 and <0.80 PARTIALLY ALIGNED; <0.60 NOT MAPPED. Without a verified reference, status is UNCLASSIFIED. These thresholds are analysis settings, not PSF standards. “Not clearly mapped to PSF-AAI” describes the selected taxonomy and method; it does not establish that the framework lacks the skill. Researchers should inspect context and adjudicate consequential mapping decisions.

## Demand and rates

For skill *s*, employer demand count is the number of distinct selected job ads containing *s*. Demand percentage is that count divided by all selected job ads, including those with no skill detections, multiplied by 100. The UI supports demand summaries by job title, location, category, PSF job role and posting date when available. Job-advertisement and employer-demand frequency bar charts use whole-number axis intervals; this display choice does not change underlying counts, percentages or model metrics.

Job-market analysis also summarizes source-provided job titles and occupational-family labels, plus explicit education and experience fields. Counts are unique ads and percentages use the selected ads as the denominator. Missing requirement fields are reported as “Not stated.” Pattern matches in qualifications, descriptions or skills text are kept as source-linked evidence candidates with surrounding text; they are not silently promoted to structured job requirements and require researcher verification.

Phrase-level aligned and partially aligned rates divide the corresponding status count by all phrases in ALIGNED or PARTIALLY ALIGNED status. The unmapped rate divides NOT MAPPED phrases by all assessed phrases (ALIGNED, PARTIALLY ALIGNED and NOT MAPPED). UNCLASSIFIED is excluded; if its denominator is empty, the rate is unavailable rather than zero. Rates are calculated before the status-display filter and metadata records their denominators.

Unmapped skills are phrases below the mapping threshold. “Emerging skill candidates” are unmapped phrases present in at least the configured number of distinct job ads (default two). This does not establish novelty or absence from PSF-AAI; that requires human review, source context and longitudinal evidence.

## Outputs and limitations

Training sources and the analytical sample serve separate purposes. Reviewed training examples may come from additional eligible job-ad sources. Findings and research exports use only the active cleaned job upload and selected filters, for example the study's selected 500-ad sample. Extraction records and exported annotations are restricted to selected job source IDs; additional training records are excluded. Export metadata records selected row count, source IDs and a sample fingerprint. Researchers must verify the retained count after screening, deduplication and filtering rather than assume that an uploaded 500-row file yields 500 eligible advertisements.

Research exports include selected cleaned job data, extracted phrases, sample-linked reviewed labels, verified PSF-AAI, classifier predictions, PSF mappings, employer–PSF alignment, demand, job-title and occupational-family counts, requirement summaries and source-linked requirement evidence, unmapped candidate tables, a workbook and a generated written report. Model evaluation and held-out test predictions are available separately through Evaluate models; they are not presented as findings from the selected analytical sample. Training on additional data does not make evaluation independent: document any overlap between training sources and the research sample. Source IDs, evidence fields and reference pages support traceability. Metrics from a saved evaluation that overlaps job-ad source IDs are withheld from the Model Evaluation page. Any displayed scores still require researcher review and independent validation before publication.

Job-ad samples may be incomplete or unrepresentative. Public ads can be duplicated, stale or ambiguous about required versus preferred skills. PDF layout extraction can omit or misread text; manual verification is required. Exact/alias matching may miss paraphrases, while fuzzy/semantic matching may overstate similarity. Classifier evaluation is conditional on human label quality and sample composition. Independent double coding, agreement/adjudication and out-of-sample evaluation strengthen research validity. Findings are descriptive and do not establish causal relationships or workforce proficiency.
