# Methodology: Philippine employer skills and PSF-AAI

## Context: research question and scope

To what extent do skills and competencies required in Philippine AI- and analytics-related job advertisements align with the Philippine Skills Framework for Analytics and Artificial Intelligence (PSF-AAI)? The analysis covers Philippine job ads and a researcher-verified PSF-AAI reference.

## Approach: system workflow at a glance

The application supports a researcher-led, descriptive comparison of job-advertised skills with the verified PSF-AAI reference. It prepares and organizes evidence; it does not determine whether the sample is representative or certify that an AI suggestion is correct.

| Stage | System role | Researcher decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare data | Map source columns, clean job records, preserve identifiers and source evidence, and flag possible duplicates. | Confirm Philippine provenance, AI/analytics relevance, inclusion, and duplicate handling. |
| Verify framework | Extract a reviewable PSF-AAI reference from the official source and retain page and source provenance. | Check competency names, descriptions, codes, roles, levels, and cited pages before activation. |
| Discover phrases | Find candidate skill phrases and retain normalized text, job-ad occurrence, and context. | Inspect context, refine phrases, and exclude irrelevant candidates. |
| Label and map | Keep analytical category labels separate from PSF-AAI links; produce model-assisted drafts and candidate matches. | Review each category and each phrase-ad mapping. Multiple competencies require separate source-based rationales. |
| Analyze and report | Rebuild results from current study inputs and reviewed occurrence decisions; calculate demand and alignment summaries. | Check denominators, source coverage, stale evidence, and limitations before interpreting or reporting results. |

AI-generated candidates remain suggestions until a researcher saves a decision. A category applies to a normalized phrase across its advertisements; a PSF-AAI decision applies only to the reviewed phrase in its specific advertisement.

## Data and units

The primary observational unit is a unique job advertisement identified by its source record ID. Researcher screening must establish Philippine provenance and AI/analytics relevance; a Philippine location alone is insufficient. The app records an explicit scope review before extraction from uploaded job data. Keep source URL, collection details and raw file hash. Confirm duplicates before cleaning, and report source coverage and sampling dates. The skill-demand unit is a distinct job ad mentioning a normalized skill phrase; repeated mentions in one ad count once.

The PSF-AAI reference stores competency name and description, functional/enabling category, official competency codes, framework career-role links and listed levels, source PDF page and URL, source hash, reviewer, review status and timestamp. When an explicitly labeled Range of Application is present in a verified reference table, it is retained as additional context for semantic candidate ranking and researcher review; it does not create another competency or independently establish a match. The bundled TESDA PDF edition has no separate Range of Application field, so that field remains blank unless supplied and verified from an authoritative source. PDF extraction produces a reviewable draft. It is not an authoritative active taxonomy until a researcher checks definitions, codes, roles and levels against their cited pages. Aliases and keywords are not inferred from prose.

This study does not collect or compare university curricula. Its comparison is employer-stated job-ad skills against the researcher-verified TESDA PSF-AAI reference.

## Job text preparation and phrase discovery

Map source columns to the app's job schema; preserve raw bytes and source fields. Cleaning normalizes HTML, Unicode and whitespace and lowercases descriptive job-analysis values, including c++, c#, .net, r, sql and power bi. Technical punctuation is retained. Raw source fields, identifiers, URL paths, dates and official framework records retain their source identity. Empty fields stay empty. A reviewer confirms duplicate removals.

The existing NLP pipeline combines exact framework terms and researcher-supplied aliases/keywords with general discovery vocabulary and spaCy lookup lemmatization over candidate n-grams. Every candidate retains its original phrase, normalized phrase, job ID and source context. Candidate limits are recorded. General vocabulary and model predictions are discovery evidence, not human labels or PSF matches. Researchers inspect context, edit phrases and may label a candidate IRRELEVANT.

## Saved results and reproducibility

A published extraction retains candidate and match records, discovery diagnostics, a creation timestamp and an input fingerprint. The fingerprint covers the cleaned job advertisements and PSF-AAI reference, active upload hashes, column mappings, saved category annotations, settings and selected-model metadata. New extraction runs also record the producing software version and an extraction-pipeline revision. The pipeline revision identifies changes in extraction, matching or classification behavior; interface changes have a separate software version.

Loading saved results retrieves existing extraction records without repeating text processing, matching, classifier inference or training. Results may be reused after compatible interface updates when their research-input fingerprint still matches. Changes to research inputs or the extraction pipeline require another extraction before the Results pages and research exports use them. The Results and Research Readiness pages apply the same freshness check. For supported older runs whose fingerprint included the application version, the system reconstructs fingerprints for explicitly compatible releases using the current research inputs and requires an exact match. Loading does not replace the original extraction timestamp or rewrite its historical metadata.

Result reuse is an operational measure for repeatable analysis and reduced processing time. It does not independently validate the source advertisements, researcher labels, classifier performance or competency mappings. Saved occurrence-level PSF-AAI reviews are incorporated when analysis tables are rebuilt; each review must still match its source evidence and reference. Updating these reviews is distinct from rerunning the extraction pipeline.

## Manual labels and supervised classification

### Analytical category hierarchy

Each normalized phrase receives one researcher-reviewed leaf category. These are study-defined analytical categories, distinct from the official PSF-AAI reference categories. Technical / Digital is the parent of AI / Machine Learning, Data / Analytics, and general programming and digital tools. Enabling capabilities contains communication and personal effectiveness (stored as Soft / Enabling) and Leadership / Management. Business / Domain represents domain knowledge. Education / Qualification is a credential requirement, not a competency. IRRELEVANT identifies excluded material. The legacy stored value Technical / Digital denotes the general programming and digital-tools leaf; the interface displays its full hierarchy.

Researchers select the most specific applicable leaf using source context and the decision rules in CATEGORY_CODEBOOK.md. Parent categories and requirement types are derived from the leaf. Parent and child totals must not be added together; job-level parent counts require deduplication within the parent. Credentials should be reported separately from competency frequencies. Existing labels are not automatically re-reviewed by changing the codebook.

### AI-assisted suggestions and researcher review

The pinned BAAI/bge-m3 embedding model (revision 5617a9f61b028005a4858fdac845db406aefb181) ranks the study's category descriptions and competencies in the active PSF-AAI taxonomy using a phrase and nearby source context. Exact project-vocabulary rules take priority for known phrases. Blank and symbol-only phrases are excluded from review display and cannot receive competency suggestions. BGE-M3 similarities are rankings, not calibrated probabilities or accuracy measures.

For each eligible phrase occurrence, the application can present one category draft and up to three ranked PSF-AAI candidates. The candidates are prompts for review, not automatic labels or approved mappings. Researchers inspect the job-ad evidence, choose the category and any applicable competencies, provide a separate rationale for each competency, and save their decisions. Similarity scores only rank candidates; they are not probabilities, accuracy estimates, or evidence that every suggested competency applies.

Only BGE-M3 suggestions and researcher review informed the study's model-assisted labeling process. No separate chat-model context check was used to generate, check, or determine study labels, mappings, or results.

Candidate embeddings are reused for category and PSF ranking, and completed draft batches are retained. These are operational measures, not evidence of improved research accuracy. BGE-M3 is not fine-tuned when researchers save annotations. The suggestion workflow requires evaluation against independently reviewed examples before claims of improved classification quality.

### Researcher review and training observations

For a new phrase, the category field begins blank; unreviewed phrase-ad occurrences have no researcher-approved PSF-AAI links. AI drafts are read-only guidance beside the researcher-controlled fields in the same review table. Researchers choose values and explicitly save; drafts are never copied into labels automatically. A category decision applies to a normalized phrase across its advertisements, while PSF-AAI links are decided for each phrase-ad occurrence and may include multiple competencies. Each selected competency requires its own source-based rationale and confirmation. Saved decisions record reviewer identity and timestamp, but the app cannot establish that a reviewer actually checked the evidence or that a decision is correct. PSF assignment is optional and remains distinct from the analytical category.

Only reviewed Philippine job-ad phrases passing provenance and conflict validation qualify for research training. A normalized phrase has one category across occurrences; researchers must resolve disagreements and refine ambiguous phrases using source context. AI drafts alone and synthetic software fixtures do not qualify as research training observations.

The explicit training action compares TF-IDF pipelines with Multinomial Naive Bayes and balanced Logistic Regression. Training prefers `normalized_phrase`, falling back to `phrase`. When source IDs are present, the holdout and cross-validation split by job-ad source ID so wording from one ad cannot appear in both training and evaluation. Training is blocked if a source-disjoint holdout cannot retain every category in both partitions. If source IDs are unavailable in a non-production software fixture, the fallback phrase-level split is explicitly identified and must not be treated as research evaluation. Model selection uses only the training partition: up to five cross-validation folds rank models by mean macro F1, then mean weighted F1, then lower macro-F1 standard deviation; original model order breaks exact ties. If valid cross-validation cannot be computed for both candidates, the run stops without publishing or replacing saved models. The winner is fixed before either candidate is scored on the held-out partition. Both candidates retain holdout diagnostics for comparison, but these scores never choose the winner, and neither saved pipeline is refitted on holdout labels. New metadata records training-cross-validation selection; older saved runs remain readable and are identified as internal comparisons until retrained. Repeatedly adjusting the method after inspecting holdout scores compromises that holdout; final reporting should use a frozen protocol and an untouched reviewed evaluation set. Artifacts include fitted pipelines and data/configuration hashes. A grouped holdout is only an internal estimate for the sampled postings; it does not validate extraction, PSF mapping, label quality, representativeness, or performance on a separately collected sample.

Operational gates require at least 30 distinct usable phrases, two classes, five phrases per class, no conflicting phrase labels, complete provenance and a feasible source-disjoint split. These are safeguards, not a claim of statistical adequacy. The Research Readiness page additionally flags source concentration, reviewer-ID variants, short phrase fragments and phrase-level evaluation leakage. It cannot establish annotation quality or a representative sampling frame. Researchers should seek independent labels, adjudicate disagreement, document inter-rater agreement, and evaluate on a genuinely independent sample. A separate `models/provisional/` artifact is reserved for weak-supervision experiments and is never used by the production classifier or reported as a research model.

## Employer-to-PSF mapping and alignment

Mapping uses a many-to-many relationship between extracted skill occurrences and competencies in the active PSF-AAI reference. An occurrence is one distinct normalized skill phrase within one job advertisement; repeated mentions of that phrase within the same advertisement count once. Several distinct phrases may map to the same competency, including phrases from different advertisements. The same phrase may have different mappings in different advertisements. Each link retains the extraction ID, source advertisement ID, original text, available character offsets, advertisement title and URL, and the target competency ID and reference evidence. Advertisement URLs and framework-reference URLs are stored separately.

The active official-PDF importer retains each competency's short definition plus the continuation-page proficiency descriptions, underpinning knowledge and skills-application text. It keeps Range of Application separately and records the competency heading page, source URL and PDF hash. Exact competency names, researcher-provided aliases/keywords, optional local semantic evidence or fuzzy similarity supply at most one automatic candidate per occurrence. Phrase matches found in the competency profile prose (including proficiency and application text) or Range of Application are marked PARTIALLY ALIGNED as supporting evidence, not as direct title matches; profile-token matching is heuristic and requires researcher review. Separately, the model-assisted manual-review interface presents up to three distinct PSF-AAI candidates ranked by BGE-M3 similarity between the phrase with nearby context and the framework competency name, description, and any verified Range of Application text. Three is an interface shortlist limit, not a validated classification threshold. Candidate names, IDs and ranking scores are persisted as AI drafts; existing single-candidate drafts remain readable. The shortlist neither assigns multiple mappings nor establishes that all listed competencies apply. Researchers independently select and justify every saved link. Additional competencies require researcher review of that occurrence. Researchers select each applicable competency, supply a separate competency-specific rationale, and confirm that the source context supports every link. Broad or ambiguous wording alone is insufficient grounds for multiple assignments. An explicit no-match decision is also supported. The interface checks for missing and duplicated rationales; it cannot establish their substantive validity, which remains the researcher's responsibility. Saving a decision changes only that occurrence. Manual Labeling presents category and multiple PSF-AAI selections together in a review table. By default, one row represents a distinct normalized phrase and displays a representative advertisement plus the number of source advertisements containing it. An alternative occurrence view allows inspection of the phrase in each advertisement. Grouping the review display does not remove source occurrences or change demand frequencies. Categories are assigned per normalized phrase, while multiple PSF-AAI links are reviewed per phrase and advertisement using the same occurrence-review store as PSF Mapping. Category-only saves preserve each source occurrence’s existing legacy PSF reference without copying it to other advertisements. Occurrence reviews take precedence in the research analysis.

Reviewed links record reviewer identity, timestamp, decision version, and hashes of the source context and active reference. Persisted PSF-AAI decisions remain visible with the selected competencies, reviewer and save time; an existing decision can be updated. An explicit reviewed no-match remains distinguishable from an unreviewed occurrence. Shared saves are reported as confirmed only when the database returns the expected new decision version; an uncertain response requires checking the persisted decision before retrying. A changed source or reference invalidates the prior decision until re-reviewed. Shared reviews use version checks to reject concurrent overwrites and retain decision history. Mapping results and exports include the PSF competency profile and, when available in the verified source, Range of Application as the visible reference basis for each comparison. Results also retain career role, reference category and listed level, source page/URL/hash, match method and similarity fields. Similarity is a heuristic, not a calibrated correctness probability. A researcher-selected exact reference is recorded as a human assignment; its exact-match score is not an independent validation metric. A listed reference level does not imply applicant proficiency. Education and qualification requirements are analyzed separately from competency demand and are excluded from the PSF skills-gap calculation.

Competency demand counts distinct advertisements, distinct skill phrases, and distinct occurrences separately. Several links from one advertisement to the same competency count as one advertisement. Competency counts overlap and must not be summed as a unique-advertisement total. Phrase-level alignment rates count each normalized phrase once, using its strongest observed status across links and advertisements (aligned, then partially aligned, then not mapped, then unclassified). This summary describes whether a phrase has supporting alignment anywhere in the selected sample; it does not imply that all its occurrences align. The occurrence mapping table preserves mixed and stale decisions for inspection. Multiple links do not increase the phrase-rate denominator.

Default thresholds are configurable: score ≥0.80 ALIGNED; ≥0.60 and <0.80 PARTIALLY ALIGNED; <0.60 NOT MAPPED. Without a verified reference, status is UNCLASSIFIED. These thresholds are analysis settings, not PSF standards. “Not clearly mapped to PSF-AAI” describes the selected taxonomy and method; it does not establish that the framework lacks the skill. Researchers should inspect context and adjudicate consequential mapping decisions.

## Demand and rates

For skill *s*, employer demand count is the number of distinct selected job ads containing *s*. Demand percentage is that count divided by all selected job ads, including those with no skill detections, multiplied by 100. The UI supports demand summaries by job title, location, category, PSF job role and posting date when available. Job-advertisement and employer-demand frequency bar charts use whole-number axis intervals; this display choice does not change underlying counts, percentages or model metrics.

Job-market analysis also summarizes job titles and occupational-family labels, plus explicit education and experience fields. A clear leading requisition code in the form of 2–6 letters, 4 or more digits, and a separator is removed from analytical title labels; the source title remains unchanged in the advertisement and title evidence. Counts are unique ads and percentages use the selected ads as the denominator. Missing requirement fields are reported as “Not stated.” Pattern matches in qualifications, descriptions or skills text are kept as source-linked evidence candidates with surrounding text; they are not silently promoted to structured job requirements and require researcher verification.

Phrase-level aligned and partially aligned rates divide the corresponding status count by all phrases in ALIGNED or PARTIALLY ALIGNED status. The unmapped rate divides NOT MAPPED phrases by all assessed phrases (ALIGNED, PARTIALLY ALIGNED and NOT MAPPED). UNCLASSIFIED is excluded; if its denominator is empty, the rate is unavailable rather than zero. Rates are calculated before the status-display filter and metadata records their denominators.

Unmapped skills are phrases below the mapping threshold. “Emerging skill candidates” are unmapped phrases present in at least the configured number of distinct job ads (default two). This does not establish novelty or absence from PSF-AAI; that requires human review, source context and longitudinal evidence.

## Outputs and limitations

Training sources and the analytical sample serve separate purposes. Reviewed training examples may come from additional eligible job-ad sources. Findings and research exports use only the active cleaned job upload and selected filters, for example the study's selected 500-ad sample. Extraction records and exported annotations are restricted to selected job source IDs; additional training records are excluded. Export metadata records selected row count, source IDs and a sample fingerprint. Researchers must verify the retained count after screening, deduplication and filtering rather than assume that an uploaded 500-row file yields 500 eligible advertisements.

Research exports include selected cleaned job data, extracted phrases, sample-linked reviewed labels, verified PSF-AAI, classifier predictions, PSF mappings, employer–PSF alignment, demand, job-title and occupational-family counts, requirement summaries and source-linked requirement evidence, unmapped candidate tables, a workbook and a generated written report. Model evaluation and held-out test predictions are available separately through Evaluate models; they are not presented as findings from the selected analytical sample. Training on additional data does not make evaluation independent: document any overlap between training sources and the research sample. Source IDs, evidence fields and reference pages support traceability. Metrics from a saved evaluation that overlaps job-ad source IDs are withheld from the Model Evaluation page. Any displayed scores still require researcher review and independent validation before publication.

Job-ad samples may be incomplete or unrepresentative. Public ads can be duplicated, stale or ambiguous about required versus preferred skills. PDF layout extraction can omit or misread text; manual verification is required. Exact/alias matching may miss paraphrases, while fuzzy/semantic matching may overstate similarity. Classifier evaluation is conditional on human label quality and sample composition. Independent double coding, agreement/adjudication and out-of-sample evaluation strengthen research validity. Findings are descriptive and do not establish causal relationships or workforce proficiency.


## Descriptive presentation for the chancellor and students

The dashboard supports two reading tasks: the chancellor can inspect observed employer-demand patterns and PSF-AAI correspondence, while students can explore advertised skills and qualifications for individual roles. It does not infer curriculum deficiencies, individual employability, future demand, or national workforce prevalence from the selected job advertisements. A short workflow precedes the results. Skills, roles and locations, qualifications, and framework demand have separate views so readers do not have to decode one overcrowded chart.

Ranked horizontal bars start at zero, use integer advertisement counts, readable labels and direct values, and default to descending demand with deterministic alphabetical tie ordering so the most frequently requested requirements appear first. Alphabetical order is available for lookup and is explicitly distinguished from ranking. The display limit is visible; full tables remain exportable. Highlighted bars indicate the highest displayed count, including ties, rather than a causal or policy recommendation. Each skill counts distinct selected ads; percentages include selected ads with no detected skills. Role and location selectors use that group's distinct-ad denominator. Cities and regions are not guessed from ambiguous location wording. Monthly posting counts use bars to describe sample-date coverage, not market growth.

A separate chart view consolidates case, Unicode, whitespace, invisible-separator and list-format variants without fuzzy synonym merging. Technical punctuation is preserved in lowercase labels such as c++, c#, .net, r, sql and power bi; the display no longer restores capitalized vocabulary names. Missing-value placeholders (including “not mentioned”), experience-duration or year fragments, credentials, confirmed irrelevant labels, obvious connector fragments, generic standalone actions, and unfinished list tails (such as “etc.” or “and others”) are withheld from skill charts and automatic PSF-AAI matching. Exact “access to benefit(s)” fragments, including the normalized form “access benefit,” are withheld pending source-context review because the wording does not identify a competency. Wording made only of vague descriptors or context nouns (such as “strong,” “advanced,” or “business experience”) is also withheld when it does not identify a competency. Standalone occupational labels (such as analyst, business analyst, engineer) and incomplete/context-only phrases (such as data, industry experience, or base produced by lemmatizing “based”) are likewise excluded; their original phrase, source evidence and saved reviewer decision remain available for audit. The chart groups the exact phrases “ability to learn” and “learn and apply” under the broader display label “learning and application.” This is an explicit umbrella label; it does not claim identical wording. Original phrases and source evidence remain available, and each advertisement counts once even if it contains both. The display alias does not change researcher labels, training data, or PSF-AAI links. Other learning-related phrases remain separate until researchers document a shared construct. These chart rules do not delete or rewrite researcher labels. The source-linked chart_phrase_review audit retains every phrase withheld by these rules, whether or not extraction marked it accepted; counts for missing placeholders, experience/year wording, irrelevant labels and ads with no chartable skill appear below the employer-skill chart instead of as categories. Explicit structured experience fields are summarized separately under Qualifications. These are transparent wording screens, not semantic certainty detection: they do not identify every ambiguous phrase or automatically convert description text into a confirmed requirement. Researchers must review the source context and decide whether to correct or exclude an ambiguous phrase. Chart demand and original alignment tables are distinct exports, and chart_view_settings records display controls without limiting full exports. Unmapped charts apply documented display aliases across equivalent phrase variants, count each ad once under the resulting label, and omit groups with mapped evidence visible in the current results; they do not establish framework absence.

Additional high-precision graph-only checks withhold the exact “protect by applicable (law)” equal-employment boilerplate, “activity/activities and work” generic duty wording, and “adoption of insight(s)” outcome/KPI wording. In the selected 500-ad source descriptions, those expressions occur in legal boilerplate, a duty statement, and a success-metrics section respectively. The chart alias maps “agile scrum,” “scrum,” and their method/methodology variants to one display label; standalone “agile” remains separate. Lemmatizer-generated “datum” is restored to “data” in chart labels because no source description in the selected 500 says “datum.” These additional checks and aliases change chart presentation only; they preserve extracted rows, researcher labels, PSF-AAI mappings and training inputs. Excluded phrases remain in the source-linked chart_phrase_review audit. The screen is deterministic and conservative; it does not claim to understand every phrase or replace researcher judgment.

Employer-skill charts share one display-alias layer. It groups communication/communication-skills wording, direct collaboration variants, and problem-solve/problem-solving variants under broader labels; each ad contributes at most once per label. Written and verbal communication, teamwork, adaptability, and other distinct constructs remain separate. The original extracted phrases, reviewed categories, training labels, and PSF-AAI links are unchanged. These aliases feed the overall demand, occupational-family, title, location, category, and unmapped/emerging chart views.

Exact phrase review also found non-skill fragments to withhold from every skill graph: “allow individuals” is company marketing about customers, “analyst work” is occupational experience wording, “assets across Strategy” is corporate profile text, “AI based” is malformed wording such as “AI based on…,” and “AI Prompt” is a title fragment. They remain visible in `chart_phrase_review` with reasons. Full job titles and complete competency statements remain in their appropriate analyses.

### Quality rules across graphs

One screened occurrence table feeds every employer-skill visualization: overall demand, category splits, occupational-family comparisons, selected-title and selected-location charts, and unmapped/emerging charts. This keeps exclusions, aliases, and distinct-ad counting consistent across skill graphs; excluded wording remains in the source-linked phrase audit. Job-title graphs omit exact missing-value placeholders, strip clear requisition codes, and merge modifiers only when one occupation is explicit. Geographic graphs include only exact localities/regions resolved by the documented lookup; missing, broad, conflicting, and unfamiliar locations remain in evidence, outside region/city comparisons. Education graphs use one unambiguous parsed credential level; experience summaries use parsed explicit source fields and keep ambiguous values for review; date charts use parseable posting dates and report missing-date coverage. PSF-AAI competency and role labels come from the active reference and existing mapping decisions; model evaluation graphs use validated label classes and computed results. These graph-specific rules preserve the meaning of each measure rather than applying a skill-word filter to unrelated labels. The 500-ad input was checked for source-label quality separately from the stale local extraction snapshot; updated phrase counts require refreshed extraction linked to the selected ads.

Education and experience coverage is stated as counts, including missing values. Missing-only or single-category fields use text or tables rather than uninformative charts. Structured experience-year summaries parse explicit source fields only, preserve the original phrase, and distinguish ranges, minimums, maximums and explicit no-experience wording. Ambiguous, malformed, age-related, mixed-unit or multi-clause expressions remain Needs review. Plain stated years describe source wording rather than an inferred maximum hiring policy. Description-text matches remain separate candidates; they do not fill missing requirement fields or establish a confirmed requirement. The raw upload and saved review evidence are unchanged by these presentation updates.

## Detailed processing protocol and standardization (v2.0.85)

The dashboard's Complete processing workflow documents ten stages: (1) archive collection and source identity; (2) researcher screening against Philippine provenance, occupational relevance and text sufficiency; (3) confirm duplicate removal; (4) normalize descriptive text to lowercase; (5) extract typed requirements with advertisement IDs; (6) classify and deduplicate skill mentions; (7) inspect PSF-AAI correspondence and justify mappings; (8) aggregate distinct-ad counts and proportions; (9) explore filtered visualizations; and (10) interpret and export evidence with limitations. Screening exclusions require reasons and are recorded in cleaning metadata. Neither automated extraction nor a model draft confirms research inclusion or a label.

Confirmed duplicates can include identical normalized title, employer, location and full description with different dates or URLs. This produces candidates for researcher confirmation; title similarity alone never merges advertisements. The most-common-title overview counts distinct advertisements under a canonical label only when one recognized occupation is explicit; clear seniority or branch/specialty modifiers can be collapsed (for example, Junior Business Analyst - Banking Branch → business analyst). Composite, ambiguous and unrecognized titles stay separate. A specific-title view retains the exact normalized wording alphabetically; source titles remain in the evidence export. Source-provider names and advertisement URLs accompany title evidence. These researcher-defined chart groups are descriptive summaries, not an official occupational classification, and they never merge advertisement records.

Education levels are recognized from explicit structured fields using documented exact wording and degree abbreviations. Only a single unambiguous level enters the ascending horizontal education chart. Multiple, alternative, preferred or unrecognized levels retain their original wording and a review flag; the program does not select the highest degree or infer a minimum. Missing education is reported below the chart as a count and percentage and does not enter as an education level. “Not specified” does not mean “not required.” Experience frequency tables retain parsed source wording and use the selected-ad denominator. The median and observed range are shown only for at least two exact, single-value numeric requirements; ranges, open-ended values, month-only expressions and ambiguous requirements are excluded from those statistics and kept as evidence. Experience groups cover no experience, less than one year, one to two years, three to five years, six to ten years and more than ten years. Only closed parsed values entirely contained in a group enter its count. These summaries do not fill missing fields using description mentions.

An explicit presentation alias dictionary consolidates powerbi/microsoft power bi into power bi and ms excel/microsoft excel into excel. It does not merge Python and R or broadly related competencies. Original normalized phrases are retained; saved labels and PSF decisions are not rewritten. Unmapped skill charts use decreasing distinct-ad demand and alphabetical ties. Competency charts can follow active PSF-AAI reference row order, with unmatched names last, or a researcher-selected demand/alphabetical view. This order is presentation metadata, not a TESDA importance rating.

Geographical grouping uses an offline exact-name subset of the Philippine Statistics Authority PSGC, verified 31 October 2026 against the [PSGC summary](https://psa.gov.ph/classification/psgc/summary) and [city listing](https://psa.gov.ph/classification/psgc/cities). It includes current regional membership, including Negros Island Region. Exact city and region aliases are consolidated. Bare Manila is resolved only to NCR; province/city ambiguity is not silently removed. Unknown, multiple, conflicting, broad/remote and unspecified locations remain explicit. Regional charts compare regions; after choosing a region, city/municipality charts show exact localities within it. Raw advertisement counts are not population- or labor-force-adjusted rates. The curated lookup is incomplete and requires researcher verification for unfamiliar addresses; it is not a geocoder. A choropleth is optional and is not used where ranked bars communicate the comparison more precisely.

The skill overview heatmap compares top skills across occupational families. Skill labels are arranged as rows and families as columns so long skill names stay readable. Each cell is the number of distinct advertisements in that family mentioning the skill; color shows the percentage of all ads in that family. Ads without chartable skills remain in the denominator, and a family with no chartable skills can therefore have zero cells. Missing/unclassifiable families are counted below the heatmap. Filtering visible families or skills does not change the family denominator. A selected family and title then show the top ten skills by distinct-ad count and share; the searchable table exposes the full selected list. Groups under ten ads are explicitly described as small and descriptive, not stable occupational estimates.

PSF-AAI reference-role demand is ranked separately. A second heatmap places competency labels in rows and reference roles in columns, comparing researcher-reviewed links across roles. Counts are distinct ads per role/competency cell; percentages use the number of selected ads linked to each role, including ads whose other competencies are hidden from the matrix. An ad can have multiple competencies and roles, so these percentages can overlap and cell/role counts are not additive to a unique-ad total. Existing ALIGNED or PARTIALLY ALIGNED decisions are displayed without certifying new mappings. Role labels come from the reviewed reference mapping, not job-title equivalence or applicant proficiency.

The region and city filters restrict their respective charts and the selected-area skill and posting-date views. Each denominator is named, and date coverage states usable and missing dates. These exploration controls do not silently narrow the complete exported dataset; global research filters define exported scope.

## Local processing-time assessment

A read-only benchmark runs the real local cleaning, duplicate detection, extraction/vocabulary/framework scans, mapping, aggregation and chart serialization functions on first-N copies at 20, 100, 250 and 500 ads. Reports include actual record/text volumes, input fingerprint, candidate limits, Python version, cache conditions and per-stage durations. Classifier inference is measured only when a trusted local model is explicitly supplied; no model is trained and no evaluation metrics are invented. Extraction and vocabulary/PSF matching share an implementation and are therefore reported as a combined stage rather than attributed artificial separate times.

The 31 October 2026 local run measured 7.33, 7.08, 10.53 and 114.78 seconds for 20, 100, 250 and 500 ads respectively. Corresponding cleaned-text volumes were 40,924; 85,897; 146,806; and 1,362,775 characters. The first sample includes initialization and subsequent samples use process caches, so these observations are not directly controlled scaling comparisons. On 500 ads, extraction and scans took 110.18 seconds, mapping 2.40 seconds, aggregation 1.47 seconds and one chart's construction/JSON serialization 0.031 seconds. No classifier was supplied. These measurements exclude database transfer, file I/O, sign-in and browser rendering; school-device and network usability still needs an end-to-end check. Performance claims must state these conditions.

The results bundle precomputes reusable summaries and is keyed by input/review/filter revisions. Only the selected Results section constructs its charts. Switching a view or plot selection does not rerun extraction or training. Default chart limits and bounded role/family heatmap selectors prevent crowded displays; complete tables remain exportable. Data-driven takeaways report observed counts and ties rather than fixed claims.


### Extraction implementation improvement and comparison

Profiling identified repeated reconstruction of spaCy Doc.text for each candidate n-gram. The implementation now retrieves that text once per document and slices the same string. A separate 20-long-ad experiment produced identical extracted records and diagnostics; the source text, character spans, candidate ordering and labels were preserved. This is a computational change without a change to the extraction protocol.

A subsequent comparison retaining the same 500 input advertisements measured 34.13 seconds overall and 29.49 seconds for extraction/matching, versus the earlier 114.78 and 110.18 seconds. Source and taxonomy fingerprints matched, and both runs produced 93,581 candidate occurrences, 7,963 accepted occurrences and 2,007 chart labels. Full baseline 500-ad frames were not retained, so full-record equality is established for the 20-long-ad experiment only; matching aggregate counts are not proof of semantic validity. Timings reflect local process/cache conditions and concurrent machine load and are not end-to-end speed guarantees.

Duplicate detection flagged 11 potential repeated advertisements in the local 500-row file under the stricter content rule. These were not removed from the active research dataset. The benchmark now retains every input row and reports candidates separately; researchers must confirm any removals in Review data quality. A separate intermediate benchmark using 489 retained records is not the controlled 500-ad comparison.
